## How to answer anything

1. **Answer first.** Give the answer, one fact, then stop. Aim for 20–30 seconds.
2. **Don't know?** Say: *"I don't know yet. Here's how I'll find out."* A panel trusts this more than a guess.
3. **Pushback?** Say: *"That's a fair concern."* Then give the fact. Never argue.
4. **Admit weak spots quickly.** Say it plainly, then say what you're doing about it.
5. **Label every number:** benchmark, simulation or projection. Never let a projection sound measured.
6. **Don't agree to terms in the room.** Say: *"I'd like to understand your standard terms and come back to you."*

## Part 3: Technology

### The AI

**★ What exactly is the AI? Did you build the models?**
> I use open models: Dinomaly and PatchCore on Meta's DINOv2, and RF-DETR for outlining defects. What I built is what makes them work in a factory: part alignment, calibrated thresholds, linking parts to machine cycles, the risk model, model operations, and the PLC and dashboard integration.

**★ What does AUROC 0.95–0.99 mean?**
> How well the model separates good parts from bad. 1.0 is perfect and 0.5 is a coin flip. We scored 0.95 to 0.99 on four categories of VisA, a public industrial benchmark. The older standard method scored 0.56 to 0.96 on the same categories.

**★ ⚠ So what percentage of defects do you actually catch?**
> It depends on the part. With thresholds set for low false rejects, it caught 90% on circuit boards and only 35% on capsules, the hardest category. AUROC looks better than the real catch rate. So we start as a second check beside inspectors, not a replacement.

**⚠ Only 4 of VisA's 12 categories? Did you pick the easy ones?**
> It's four of twelve, and the full run isn't done yet. The 35% result is in that set, so it isn't only the easy ones. Treat these as indicative. Running all of VisA and MVTec is on my list.

**⚠ Your simulator, your results. Why should I believe them?**
> You shouldn't treat them as proof. Simulation shows the logic works; it doesn't show real accuracy. That's why every number is labelled, and why this money goes to real hardware.

**⚠ 100% recall on segmentation sounds too good.**
> It is, for real life. That's on synthetic defects with labelled examples, so it shows the pipeline works, not field accuracy.

**What are "calibrated false rejects"?**
> Most tools set the threshold by eye. We set it statistically, using conformal calibration on good parts held back from training. It gives a stated limit on false rejects before go-live. On a simulated six-camera station, 2 of 400 unseen good parts were falsely rejected, against a 1% target.

**⚠ What does conformal calibration assume? When does it break?**
> It assumes new parts look like the calibration parts. If lighting, material or the process changes, the guarantee weakens. That's why there's drift monitoring, and recalibration goes through an approval step.

**Why does false-reject rate matter so much?**
> It's what gets AI switched off in factories. Reject too many good parts and operators stop trusting it within days.

**How many good parts do you need?**
> Several hundred, and no defect samples for detection. With 300 parts, our lighter model scored AUROC 0.946, against 0.960 with the full set. Calibrating a six-camera station needs at least 600 more. Siemens Inspekto claims 20–30 samples, so reducing this is on my list.

**What about defects you've never seen?**
> That's the strength of learning from good parts: anything unusual gets flagged. Naming the defect type needs labelled examples, and the review queue collects them.

**What can't it detect?**
> Very small defects below what the camera resolves, hidden internal defects, and logical errors, like a correct-looking part in the wrong place. Logical checks are on the roadmap.

**What's the smallest defect you can see?**
> Reliably about 6 pixels. Finding a 150-micron defect needs about 12 megapixels over a 100 mm view, or splitting the image into tiles. Real optics will set the true limit.

**How long does training take? Does it need internet?**
> It trains on an on-site GPU, in minutes for PatchCore and longer for Dinomaly. It needs no internet: models are bundled, and I verified that with an empty download cache.

**★ What if lighting changes, the part moves or the camera gets dirty?**
> Alignment handles position: at 8° rotation, false rejects went from 100% to 0% with defects still caught, and 15% remain at 15°, on simulated parts. A blurred or noisy image is flagged not inspected, never passed. Drift alerts catch slower changes. Fixed lighting in an enclosure is part of the station.

**Shiny metal parts or reflections?**
> That's mostly an optics and lighting problem, and I haven't tested it on real parts. It's one reason the test rig comes first.

**How do you handle a new part or changeover?**
> Each part gets its own recipe and model. Learning a new part means collecting good samples and calibrating. I haven't timed this on a real line.

**How fast is it?**
> On a laptop GPU, 28 ms per image and about 170 ms for a six-view part, plus roughly 75 ms to save audit records. Jetson speed is unknown until the month-3 benchmark.

**⚠ Your deck says 16.5 ms. Now you say 28?**
> Two different models. 16.5 ms is defect outlining. 28 ms is anomaly detection with part alignment.

**Why not ChatGPT or a cloud API?**
> Many plants, especially defense suppliers, can't send images outside. And a line can't wait on a network.

**Why NVIDIA and Jetson?**
> Best-supported toolkit for fast industrial AI, and cheap enough for a ₹2–2.5L station. It's the planned target, but it isn't benchmarked yet.

### Linking parts to machine data

**★ What does "linking each defect to its machine cycle" mean?**
> By the time a camera sees a defect, the machine has moved on. So we match each part to the exact cycle that made it, using a part ID, a counter or a measured delay. That cycle's pressure, temperature and load curves then show what went wrong.

**How do you get machine data?**
> Over Modbus TCP and OPC UA, which many modern machines and PLCs expose. MTConnect, FANUC FOCAS and accelerometers are planned for the next 6 months.

**⚠ What about older machines with no data port?**
> The camera still works alone. For machine data we'd add sensors, but accelerometer input isn't built yet.

**⚠ What about fast signals?**
> We read about 20 Hz today, fine for moulding-style trends. Stamping, welding and die casting need high-speed capture, which is on the roadmap.

**What if a part gets mixed up between machine and camera?**
> On a multi-camera station, a part with no matching cycle can fail but never pass. And on time-linked stations, if the machine finished a cycle and no camera reported its part, that part is flagged not inspected. Part IDs are the most reliable link where a plant has them.

**★ Can you predict defects before they happen?**
> Not reliably yet, and I won't claim it. In simulation, the model warned before only 16 of 60 defect episodes. The stronger result is approval-gated setpoint fixes, which cut scrap 79% in simulation. Early warning needs real data.

**⚠ 79% scrap reduction? Really?**
> In my own simulator only. It shows the closed loop works, not what a plant will see. Real pilots will give the real number.

### Software quality

**Is it production-grade?**
> Not yet. 166 automated tests pass, including forced failures like dead cameras, a PLC outage and power cuts. It has shadow mode, rollback and an audit log. But it hasn't run in production.

**⚠ How long has it run continuously?**
> Only short runs so far. A long soak test is still to finish. Long-run stability is something the test rig will prove.

**⚠ Is it secure?**

**Can we see it?**

**What is synthetic data, and why use it?**
> Parts and defects generated by software. I don't have real factory data yet, so it let me test the whole pipeline. It can't replace real data.

## Part 4: Safety, PLC and factory reality

**★ What if your system stops a line or misses a critical defect?**
> It can't stop a line itself. It's advisory only: it writes one verdict per part to PLC registers, and the plant's own logic decides. In pilots it runs in shadow mode beside inspectors, so nothing changes until the plant trusts the numbers.

**What happens if a camera or your software dies?**
> The PLC gets a heartbeat. If a camera goes silent, a view can't inspect, or a verdict doesn't arrive, the heartbeat stops, so the plant knows parts aren't being inspected. A part is never passed by default. I've tested this against a simulated PLC over a network, not a real one.

**Does it work with Siemens, Allen-Bradley or Mitsubishi PLCs?**
> It writes verdicts over Modbus TCP today. Any PLC that speaks Modbus TCP should work, but I haven't tested a real one. Other PLCs may need a gateway. I'd confirm per site.

**Do you need ISO 13849 safety certification?**
> It's designed to stay outside the safety-related control system, so existing validation stays intact. We confirm that with each plant's safety engineer.

**Where does the data go?**
> It stays in the plant. Everything runs on-premise with no cloud dependency, which matters for defense and aerospace suppliers.

**What's the audit log for?**
> Every verdict is recorded with a keyed hash chain, so tampering is detectable, even after a restart. Automotive and aerospace quality audits ask for that traceability.

**Is it certified for IATF 16949 or AS9100?**
> No. It's built to support those audits, and it isn't a certification. Audit-report packs are on the 12–18 month roadmap.

**Who's liable if a bad part escapes?**
> The plant's quality system stays responsible, because we're an additional check. Contracts would cap liability. I'd take legal advice before the first paid pilot.

**Will it replace inspectors?**
> Not at first. It runs beside them. Long term it moves people from repetitive checking to judgement, rework and root cause.

**What will a plant let you connect to?**
> That's a real risk: IT and quality teams are cautious. The first step asks for no line changes, just a camera and shadow mode. Machine data comes later.

**1–2 day install: have you ever done one?**
> No, it's a target. The pilots will measure it.

> Accuracy on real parts falling short, or plants not sharing machine data. Both get tested in 9 months. If machine data is hard to get, inspection still stands alone.

**What if the pilot fails?**
> I'll know exactly why: catch rate, false rejects, data access or price. dhaRti's money stops at the last tranche reached.

**What's your second-biggest risk?**
> Being a solo founder with limited factory experience. Hiring and mentors address it.

**What would make you pivot?**
> If plants see value but won't pay ₹20k, I'd test a cheaper vision-only product. If machine data is blocked, I'd go vision-first with add-on sensors.

**What if real accuracy is much worse than your benchmark?**
> I expect it to be worse at first. The first tranche finds that out cheaply, before plant installs.

**What's not working today?**
> Early warning is weak, catch rate varies a lot by part, and nothing has run on real hardware.

## Part 11: Curveballs and pressure

- **"This looks like a college project."** → "Today, it's a working prototype. The question is whether it survives a real plant, and that's what I'm asking you to help test."
- **"What did you learn that surprised you?"** → "False rejects, not accuracy, kill factory AI. And my own failure tests found serious bugs that normal runs had hidden."
- **"Tell me about a time you were wrong."** → "I thought the software was finished. When I deliberately broke it, it was telling the PLC everything was healthy with all cameras dead. I fixed it, and now I test failures first."
- **"Why should we believe your numbers?"** → "Every number is labelled benchmark, simulation or projection. Pilots replace them with measurements."
- **"What would you do with ₹50 lakh?"** → "Same plan, more stations at once, and a co-founder. But ₹15L is enough to prove it first."
- **"What if we say no?"** → "I'd ask what would change your mind, and keep going with the test rig and the conversations."
- **"Come back when you have a signed pilot."** → "That's fair. Would an introduction, or lab access alone, help me get there faster?"
- **"Why not just get a job?"** *(Your real reason.)*
- **"Who else have you told about this?"** *(Answer truthfully.)*
- **"Where will you be in 5 years?"** → "The default quality layer for Indian mid-size plants, starting from 2,000 lines." *(Say it as a goal.)*
